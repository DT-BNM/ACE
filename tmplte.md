## **Key Observations from RFI Assessment** 

3. The RFI assessment indicates that the requisite expertise resides primarily with foreign law firms. While the higher-scoring firms, predominantly foreign firms, demonstrated stronger direct regulatory capabilities and more relevant central bank and sovereign-sector experience, these strengths were generally reflected in higher estimated fees. Local firms, by comparison, would primarily perform a coordination role, with the substantive regulatory advice being delivered by their foreign partner firms. As the local firms would not assume liability for such advice, the Bank's recourse may be constrained in the event of deficiencies or disputes. This arrangement may also result in additional coordination fees of between RM10,000 and RM55,000, although two of the four local firms did not separately identify such costs. Accordingly, the RFP stage will place greater emphasis on the direct assessment of the shortlisted foreign firms, including their technical capabilities, proposed engagement teams and accountability arrangements. Detailed firmspecific findings are set out in Appendix 3.

## **_Assessment Criteria and Methodology_** 

4. The RFI responses were assessed using technical-price separation approach, whereby technical submissions were evaluated independently from their pricing proposals. This methodology was adopted to ensure that the assessment of each firm's expertise, capability, and suitability was not unduly influenced by pricing considerations. For the technical assessment, firms were evaluated against the following criteria and weightages using a rating scale of 1 to 5 (detailed scoring criteria are in Appendix 2.).

## **_Rationale for Disqualification of Local Firms_**<sup>**_7_**</sup> **_(4)_** 

**8.** While Christopher & Lee Ong demonstrated access to relevant US and EU regulatory expertise through its international partner firms, the firm was not recommended for shortlisting due to its limited in-house US and EU regulatory capabilities and the absence of demonstrated central bank advisory experience. Similarly, Albar & Partners, Zul Rafique & Partners, Skrine and Shearn Delamore & Co. were not recommended for shortlisting as their proposed approach relied substantially on external foreign counsel, with limited evidence of direct expertise in the relevant jurisdictions, established regulatory relationships, and comparable experience advising central banks or reserve managers.
